RegRadar
Project Stream: Agentic AI / Generative AI / System Programming / Cyber Security

Problem Statement
Modern hyperscale cloud architectures depend on declarative Infrastructure-as-Code (IaC)
frameworks (e.g., HashiCorp Terraform) to define, provision, and manage complex multi-tier
topologies. Simultaneously, enterprises must strictly adhere to regulatory mandates such as
HIPAA, GDPR, and NIST SP 800-53. Translating high-level legal statutes into low-level
declarative configuration syntax remains an error-prone, manual bottleneck.

Objectives










AST Parsing and Dependency DAG Generation: To build a deterministic ingestion
pipeline that parses raw declarative configurations (Terraform HCL) into normalized
Abstract Syntax Trees (AST) and compiles a Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) explicitly
modeling cross-resource bindings, data references, and parent-child hierarchies.
Centrality-Based Blast-Radius Risk Bounding: To formulate a graph-theoretic risk gate
using betweenness and eigenvector centrality metrics across the DAG to algorithmically
evaluate blast-radius risk and constrain automated remediation strictly to safe, low-centrality
leaf attributes.
Ontological Multi-Agent GraphRAG Remediation: To construct an ontological
regulatory knowledge graph mapping legal mandates (HIPAA, GDPR) to IaC configuration
requirements, driving a collaborative multi-agent swarm that synthesizes minimal,
semantically valid HCL patches.
Sandboxed Dry-Run Invariant Enforcement: To design an isolated pre-flight execution
sandbox that simulates patch deployment against ephemeral state engines to inspect plan
diffs and deterministically reject any patch violating the zero-destruction invariant ( forces
replacement, schema drop, or resource recreation).
Empirical Benchmarking and Evaluation: To evaluate the engine across diverse,
deliberately vulnerable enterprise cloud architectures, measuring compliance remediation
rate, state preservation fidelity, and reduction of manual developer remediation latency.

Scope of the Project
Societal Impact
Cloud infrastructures host the backbone of modern society, containing sensitive personal health
information, financial transactions, and civic records. By autonomously enforcing strict regulatory
baselines (such as HIPAA and GDPR encryption and zero-trust controls), RegRadar prevents
systemic data leaks and credential exposures. Furthermore, by guaranteeing zero-destruction
remediation, the platform protects essential public digital services—such as healthcare portals, digital
banking, and emergency communications—from catastrophic downtime caused by flawed
administrative automation.
Sustainability
Automated deployment failures in hyperscale cloud environments trigger redundant provisioning,
teardown, and redeployment cycles across thousands of virtualized compute instances. Empirical data
shows that 37% of declarative IaC bugs result in abrupt deployment crashes. By mathematically
bounding blast radiuses and validating configuration diffs prior to cloud API execution, RegRadar
eliminates wasted server compute hours and unnecessary continuous integration (CI) rerun churn.
This directly aligns with UN Sustainable Development Goal (SDG) 12 (Responsible Consumption
and Production) and SDG 9 (Industry, Innovation, and Infrastructure) by reducing data center
energy waste and reinforcing digital infrastructure resilience.
Market Analysis
The global Cloud Security Posture Management (CSPM) and DevSecOps market is expanding
rapidly, projected to exceed $15 billion as enterprises transition from on-premise infrastructure to
cloud-native paradigms. However, existing commercial platforms (such as Palo Alto Prisma Cloud,
Wiz, and Snyk) are predominantly diagnostic: they alert platform teams to vulnerabilities but leave
remediation backlogs to overburdened engineers. Simultaneously, enterprises are hesitant to adopt
generic AI code assistants due to hallucination risks and configuration drift. RegRadar addresses a
high-value market gap: a self-healing governance engine that provides enterprise leaders with
verifiable safety bounds, eliminating hundreds of manual engineering hours per sprint while ensuring
mathematical guarantees against production disruption.

Literature Survey
Related Works
The research domain of automated cloud compliance and declarative program repair spans four
intersecting areas: empirical IaC fault analysis, static compliance linting, generative AI code
synthesis, and graph-theoretic invariant verification.
1. Empirical Fault Taxonomies in Declarative IaC
Modern infrastructure-as-code behaves as operational software requiring formal verification. Drosos
et al. (2024) [1] conducted an empirical investigation of 360 real-world IaC bugs across production
ecosystems, revealing that 45% of defects originate from flawed logic regarding system interactions
and environment state manipulation, while 37% directly induce abrupt deployment crashes.
Extending vulnerability classification, War, Nikiema, et al. (2025) [2] cataloged 62 fine-grained
security smells across seven IaC ecosystems, documenting that security misconfigurations persist
across version control repositories for extended lifecycles because developers lack automated,
context-aware remediation frameworks. Majeed et al. (2025) [3] empirically evaluated large-scale
cloud outages and demonstrated that over 52% of configuration-induced production downtimes are
caused by unvalidated automated mutations that trigger destructive state resets.
2. Static Compliance Linters and Intermediate Representations
Static analysis tools are widely integrated into CI/CD pipelines to enforce security policies. Verdet
et al. (2024) [4] evaluated 812 enterprise Terraform repositories using Checkov, revealing that static
scanners leave extensive compliance gaps across multi-cloud environments due to their inability to
parse dynamic references and module boundaries. To overcome technology-specific syntax silos,
Saavedra et al. (2025) [5] introduced cross-platform Intermediate Representations (IR) for cloud
configurations, proving that abstract graph IRs achieve significantly higher detection precision than
regex linters. In a comparative evaluation of enterprise DevSecOps pipelines, Rahman et al. (2024)
[6] established that static policy engines generate false-positive rates exceeding 34% when evaluating
deeply nested configurations, underscoring the necessity of semantic enrichment.
3. LLM Hallucinations and Automated Program Repair (APR) in IaC
Automating configuration repair using generative models introduces severe operational risks.
Alshammari et al. (2024) [7] evaluated code-generation LLMs (including GPT-4 and Claude) on
cloud templates, finding that generative agents hallucinate invalid resource parameters in 28% of
cases and frequently synthesize breaking schema definitions. Addressing detection limitations, War,
Rawass, et al. (2025) [8] demonstrated that pairing structural Abstract Syntax Tree representations
with LLM semantic reasoning outperforms keyword matching in identifying complex architectural
misconfigurations. In the domain of template-based automated repair, Kumara et al. (2024) [9]
applied Graph Neural Encoders to synthesize configuration patches, but their framework was
restricted to rigid templates and lacked awareness of real-time state drift. Chen and Wang (2025)
[10] showed that multi-agent cooperative swarms reduce single-agent hallucination rates by dividing
code synthesis into role-specific auditing, drafting, and reviewing phases.

4. Blast-Radius Modeling, Knowledge Graphs, and Runtime Invariants
Constraining automated transformations requires formal topological modeling. Schwarz et al. (2024)
[11] introduced directed dependency graphs to quantify cascading blast radiuses during cloud
infrastructure failures, demonstrating that graph centrality directly correlates with cascading outage
vulnerability. Bhandari et al. (2024) [12] evaluated continuous compliance drift across multi-tenant
environments, showing that reactive runtime auditing fails to prevent vulnerabilities from persisting
during the deployment window. For regulatory interpretation, Zhang et al. (2024) [13] developed
GraphRAG frameworks that ground LLMs in legal ontologies, dramatically improving precision
when mapping textual mandates to technical controls. In software verification, Liu et al. (2025) [14]
formulated runtime invariant sandboxing for automated program repair, though their implementation
was limited to imperative codebases. Finally, Venkatesh et al. (2026) [15] validated the neurosymbolic governance paradigm by showing that deterministic program dependency graphs can
effectively bound generative AI agent operations in mission-critical environments.

Gap Analysis
The Detection vs. Remediation Chasm [1, 2, 4, 6]: Current research heavily emphasizes
misconfiguration detection and smell classification, but provides no closed-loop mechanism
to automatically repair identified violations in version-controlled declarative code.
Blast-Radius Ignorance in Automated Code Generation [3, 7, 9, 11]: Existing generative
AI tools and APR frameworks mutate code in isolation without evaluating how resource
edits affect the broader topology, frequently compromising shared foundational
infrastructure.
Absence of Pre-Flight State-Preservation Invariants [1, 7, 14, 15]: General software
repair tools verify syntax and unit tests, but declarative IaC requires verifying against
execution plans to prevent state drops (forces replacement) and silent cascade deletions.
Semantic Disconnect in Regulatory Parsing [8, 10, 12, 13]: Traditional rule engines fail
to capture contextual regulatory intent (such as conditional zero-trust or continuous audit
requirements), while standalone LLMs hallucinate non-existent compliance parameters.

Societal Impact

Safeguarding Citizen Privacy: By autonomously auditing and enforcing HIPAA and
GDPR compliance (e.g., compulsory AES-256 encryption at rest, TLS 1.3 in transit, and
immutable access logging), RegRadar eliminates the common misconfiguration vectors
responsible for major health and consumer data leaks.
Protecting Critical Digital Services from Outages: High-frequency automated updates
often introduce latent state mutations that crash operational cloud workloads. By enforcing
mathematical zero-destruction invariants, RegRadar guarantees that security fixes will never
bring down hospital management systems, banking switches, or public transit infrastructure.
Democratizing Enterprise-Grade Security for Non-Profits and Startups: Small
organizations handling critical user data rarely possess dedicated SecOps teams to navigate
thousands of pages of regulatory mandates. RegRadar provides autonomous,
mathematically bounded remediation out-of-the-box, eliminating prohibitive cybersecurity
consulting overhead.
